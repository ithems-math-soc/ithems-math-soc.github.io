Every Friday around lunchtime, RIKEN iTHEMS hosts a Coffee Meeting. Each meeting starts with a short presentation introducing a timely topic.

For my talk, I chose “River Networks,” inspired by the recent headlines about typhoon-related flooding in the Kanto region, including levee breaches around Lake Inba. I explored why these problems occur through mathematical ideas connected to my research.

You can find the talk title and abstract below. If you’re interested, feel free to get in touch!

============================================================

**Title**

The Structure of River Networks

**Abstract**

As the rise of the earliest civilizations shows, rivers have always been deeply connected to human society. They support agriculture and transportation, but they can also cause devastating floods. Because a river basin functions as one interconnected system, changes to one part of a river can have consequences throughout the basin, sometimes making disasters even worse. Flood control and water management therefore need to consider the entire basin as a river network, alongside its individual tributaries.

In this talk, I’ll briefly explore the unique history of rivers in southern Kanto, where we live, and introduce mathematical approaches to describing river networks and the patterns and laws found in natural river systems. Finally, I’ll discuss how these approaches might extend to other fields—including bonsai!
